Cantor makes songs with models that run on a computer you own, and plays them
on your phone. Nothing in the cloud writes your music, keeps your library, or
hears your prompts.

## Two halves

**The node** is a small program on your computer, or on any machine you trust.
It holds the models, writes the songs, and keeps your library. It runs on Linux
and macOS; a GPU makes it fast, and a CPU is enough to start.

**The app** is a field of every song you have, each drawn from its own recipe,
so you know a song by its shape before you read its name. From it you ask for a
song, a version of one, a longer one, or one that begins with your own humming.

## What sits between them

Your phone and your node find each other through a relay. The relay only
splices bytes: everything between the app and the node is encrypted end to end,
so it cannot read a prompt, a title, a lyric, or a note of audio. It does see
that two devices are talking, when, and how much.
